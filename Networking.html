Industrial IP Surveillance Network — Chemical & Processing Plant

Structured Cabling & IP Camera Deployment — CAT5e FTP / NVR, 2026

IT Networks · Industrial · IP Surveillance

Installing a surveillance network in an office building and installing one in a chemical processing plant are two different engineering problems. The environment is harsher, the heights are greater, the machinery is active, and the safety constraints are non-negotiable from the moment you arrive on site.

This project involved the full deployment of an IP camera surveillance network across the production and exterior areas of a large chemical and processing facility; covering indoor production floors where robotic palletising arms and industrial conveyors operate continuously, and outdoor areas along the building's upper facade where camera positions required working at significant height.

The cabling infrastructure was built on CAT5e FTP structured cabling, chosen for its shielded construction to maintain signal integrity in an electromagnetic environment typical of heavy industrial machinery. Cable runs were planned and routed through the facility to reach each camera position; across production ceilings, down exterior walls, and into the network rack housing the on-site NVR that serves as the central recording and monitoring point for the entire system.

The most technically demanding aspect of the installation was the outdoor facade work. Camera positions on the upper exterior of the plant building required the use of an articulated aerial work platform (cherry picker) to reach mounting heights inaccessible by ladder. All at-height work was conducted under strict industrial site safety procedures; PPE, two-person working discipline with a ground-level spotter, and coordination with the plant's own safety officer before the platform was raised at each position. The cable run at each exterior point was fed down the building facade and routed inside to the rack.

Each IP camera was configured on the local TCP/IP network after mounting; assigned a static address within the surveillance VLAN, connected to the NVR, and verified for image quality, viewing angle, and recording continuity before the position was signed off. Network connectivity and signal quality were tested and measured at each camera drop using dedicated tools before the run was terminated in the rack. The completed system delivered full coverage of the production floor, robotic work cells, and exterior perimeter, all feeding into a single on-site NVR with full-time recording and remote monitoring capability.

Solar-Powered Cloud Surveillance System — Off-Grid Installation & Field Maintenance

IP Camera · Solar Power · Cloud Recording — Steca Solsum 1010 / NetBox, 2024–2026

IT Networks · Surveillance · Solar Power

Some surveillance requirements exist precisely where infrastructure does not. No mains power. No nearby network cabinet. Just open terrain, a security need, and the expectation that the camera will be recording and uploading to the cloud regardless of the time of day or the season.

In 2024, we designed and deployed a fully autonomous solar-powered IP surveillance station for a client with exactly this requirement. The system integrates three layers that must work together without human intervention: power generation and storage, local electronics management, and cloud-connected video recording.

The photovoltaic panel mounted at the top of the mast serves as the primary power source during daylight hours, simultaneously powering the camera and the NetBox cloud recorder while channelling surplus energy into a 12V sealed lead-acid battery housed in the weatherproof enclosure below. At dusk, the Steca Solsum 1010 solar charge controller detects the drop in panel output and automatically switches the load to battery power, ensuring the camera remains operational throughout the night without any manual intervention. At dawn, the cycle reverses; solar takes over, the battery begins recharging, and the system returns to its daytime configuration autonomously.

The network side of the installation was equally deliberate. The camera was configured with a static IP address and connected to the NetBox cloud recording unit via a local TCP/IP link; the NetBox handling cellular or network uplink to the company's cloud storage platform so that footage is accessible remotely in real time without requiring on-site NVR hardware. All device addressing, stream parameters, and recording schedules were set and verified before the enclosure was sealed and the system handed over as live.

In 2026, the client reported a system outage; the camera had gone dark overnight and was not recovering during the day. Working under time pressure with a surveillance gap already open, the team drove to site and began a structured fault-finding process. Voltage measurements across the power circuit quickly pointed to the charge controller; the Steca Solsum 1010 was producing no output to the load terminals despite the panel generating correctly, confirming an internal controller fault rather than a panel, battery, or wiring issue. The faulty unit was removed from the enclosure in the field, a replacement controller was fitted and wired, and the system was brought back online; camera reconnected, cloud recording confirmed active, and the circuit verified across all three terminals before the enclosure was closed. The maintenance visit was closed with a completed service report and ticket logged in the company's field management system.

AI Health Screening Deployment — Government School Convention

Smart Device Integration & Field Installation — Longse FK02GYW / Multi-Site, 2025

IT Networks · Smart Devices · Field Service

Controlling the spread of infection in a school environment is not a software problem. It is a physical one; hundreds of pupils passing through the same entry gates every morning, with no practical way to screen each one quickly enough to avoid a bottleneck. The solution needs to be fast, non-invasive, and reliable enough to operate unattended across multiple sites simultaneously.

Under a formal government convention, our team was commissioned to deploy the Longse Smart Device 2000 FK02GYW, an AI-powered face recognition and contactless temperature measurement terminal, across several schools in the region. The objective was clear: equip every entry gate with an automated health screening point capable of identifying pupils with elevated body temperature in real time, issuing a fever alert before they entered the building, and reducing the risk of infection spreading through the student population.

The FK02GYW combines a deep learning face recognition engine with an integrated infrared thermal sensor, performing contactless face temperature measurement in under a second and triggering an audible and visual fever warning if the reading exceeds the configured threshold. The device communicates over the local network via RJ45 Ethernet and supports peripheral integration through RS485 serial interfaces, allowing it to interface with access control systems and gate barriers where required.

At each school, the installation began with the physical layer: running the necessary data cabling and electrical wiring to every entry gate position, providing each unit with a clean power feed and a network connection. The devices were then mounted at each gate on their adjustable pedestal stands, positioned at the correct height for reliable face detection across the range of pupil ages present at the site. Each unit was commissioned individually; network configuration, temperature threshold calibration, and fever alert parameters were set and verified before the device was handed over to the school administration as operational.

Across all sites, the deployment produced a consistent, automated first line of health screening at every school entry point; a system that works silently in the background every morning, without requiring staff intervention, and flags the cases that need attention before they reach the classroom.

Radar Speed Warning Display Deployment — Public Road Infrastructure

Smart Road Equipment & Field Installation — Dahua DHI-ITACS-024SA-JB, 2025

IT Networks · Smart Infrastructure · Field Service

Speed limit signs tell drivers what the limit is. Radar speed warning displays tell them what they are actually doing — and that difference, in the seconds before a driver decides to brake, is where road safety interventions have their most immediate effect.

This project involved the deployment of Dahua Radar Speed Warning Screens (DHI-ITACS-024SA-JB) across several road entry points outside urban areas; locations where vehicle speeds tend to be highest and where a passive sign alone is the least effective tool available. The DHI-ITACS-024SA-JB operates on a 24.150 GHz radar centre frequency, detecting vehicle speeds from 5 to 199 km/h with an accuracy rating of ±1 km/h, and displaying the reading in real time on a large-format LED matrix panel visible from a significant approach distance.

Every panel was tested and verified before installation; the internal electronics were inspected, the LED display matrix powered and validated, and the radar detection function confirmed to be operating correctly. This pre-installation testing discipline ensures that no fault is discovered after a unit is already mounted at height on a road pole, where fault-finding becomes significantly more complex and time-consuming.

Installation was carried out at road-side poles in live traffic environments; each unit was secured to the pole with stainless steel banding, all necessary 220V AC electrical wiring was run and connected to the unit's power input, and the panel was aligned toward the approaching traffic lane to maximise radar detection range and LED display visibility. Working at height on an active road required strict adherence to safety procedures throughout; ladder positioning, traffic awareness, and two-person coordination were maintained at every installation point.

The completed installations deliver a permanent, automated speed awareness tool at each road entry point; one that requires no operator presence, responds to every vehicle that passes, and provides drivers with the one piece of information most likely to change their behaviour in the moment.

Full-Building IT Infrastructure Deployment — 8-Floor Commercial Office Tower

Structured Cabling & Network Infrastructure — CAT6 / Rack Servers / Patch Panels, 2025

IT Networks · Structured Cabling · Field Service

When a building has eight floors and no network infrastructure at all, the work does not begin at a screen. It begins at the ground floor with a cable reel, a plan, and the full weight of knowing that every decision made in the walls today will determine how the building communicates for years to come.

As lead technician on this project, I coordinated a small team to design and deploy the complete IT infrastructure of a commercial office building from the ground up, floor by floor, across all eight levels. The scope covered every layer of the physical network: cable pulling, conduit routing, rack installation, patch panel termination, labelling, and connectivity validation before handover to the client.

The cabling backbone of the project was CAT6 structured cabling, pulled through corrugated conduits and metal cable trays routed along ceilings and down partition walls throughout each floor. Every run was planned to avoid interference with the building's electrical and plumbing routes, and every conduit exit point was positioned ahead of the partition wall finishing, so that the cable infrastructure was embedded cleanly within the building structure rather than surface-mounted as an afterthought. The photos tell the story of that in-wall phase; open metal stud frames, corrugated conduits bundled and labelled at junction boxes, cable runs descending through floor penetrations to the next level down, and ceiling tray sections packed with data and power feeds moving across the open slab before partitioning closed everything in.

Each of the eight floors received its own dedicated rack server enclosure, housing a patch panel terminating all horizontal cable runs from that floor's outlet points. This per-floor distribution design keeps fault domains contained, makes future moves and adds straightforward, and gives the building a fully managed network architecture rather than a centralised star topology that would have required significantly longer cable runs from a single point.

Every cable run was labelled and documented at both ends throughout the installation, not at the end of it; a discipline that saved significant time during the termination and testing phase. Connectivity was validated at each floor before the team moved on, catching any physical layer faults while the walls were still accessible. The project was completed with full technical documentation covering the cabling schedule, rack layouts, patch panel assignments, and floor-by-floor test records, handed over as part of the building's IT commissioning package.

UniFi Wi-Fi Network Deployment — Multi-Room Commercial Installation

Field Network Engineering — Ubiquiti UniFi 6 / Cloud Gateway Ultra, 2025

IT Networks · Wi-Fi · Field Service

Most network projects start on a screen — topology diagrams, IP plans, configuration templates. This one started on a floor plan and a cable reel.

The client was a retail business operating across multiple rooms on a single floor, struggling with inconsistent wireless coverage and a network infrastructure that had grown organically without any real architecture behind it. The brief was straightforward: deliver reliable, managed Wi-Fi across every corner of the space — and do it cleanly, without disrupting day-to-day operations.

The solution was built around the Ubiquiti UniFi ecosystem: three UniFi 6 Professional access points for dual-band wireless coverage, anchored by a UniFi Cloud Gateway Ultra as the network controller and router. Before any device was mounted or configured, the physical layer had to be right — which meant pulling and routing CAT5e FTP cabling through the space, terminating every run cleanly, and validating each drop with a cable tester before anything powered on. Connectivity that isn't verified at the physical layer will always cause problems at the logical layer.

With the cabling qualified, the UniFi stack was brought online. The Cloud Gateway Ultra was commissioned as the network core — handling routing, DHCP, and cloud-managed controller functions from a single compact unit. Each access point was adopted into the UniFi Network application, configured with consistent SSIDs, band steering, and transmission power settings tuned for the room layout, then ceiling-mounted at positions chosen to eliminate dead zones without creating unnecessary channel overlap between units.

The final network delivered seamless roaming across all rooms, centralised visibility through the UniFi dashboard, and a clean physical installation with no exposed cable runs. The project was closed with a completed service report and ticket closure in the company's field service management system — documenting the topology, device MACs, IP assignments, and test results for the client's records.

ANPR Road Surveillance System — Government Infrastructure Deployment

Field Network Engineering — Dahua Smart ANPR / PoE / Cloud Recording, 2025

IT Networks · Surveillance · Field Service

There are projects where the margin for error is near zero. When the client is a government agency and the system being deployed controls vehicle access on a public road in a rural area, every decision made on site carries real operational weight.

This project was carried out in collaboration with the CAT service, deploying a complete Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR) surveillance system at a government-managed road control point. The scope covered the full deployment lifecycle: driving to the remote site, assessing the installation environment, mounting and cabling the cameras, assembling the rack infrastructure, and commissioning the entire system end to end.

The camera layer was built around two Dahua Smart ANPR cameras (DHI-ITC413-PW4D-IZ1), positioned at the road entry and exit points to capture and read vehicle number plates in real time under outdoor conditions. A third face detection camera was installed inside the control office to cover personnel identification at the checkpoint. All three units are PoE-powered, which meant the entire camera layer ran over a single structured cabling infrastructure with no separate power wiring required at each mount point.

The back-end infrastructure was built inside a network rack assembled and wired on site: a PoE switch to power and connect all camera feeds, and a NET Box cloud recording unit to handle continuous video storage and remote access for the supervising authority. TCP/IP addressing and port configuration were applied to each device to ensure clean communication between the cameras, the recorder, and the remote monitoring platform. Basic cybersecurity checks were carried out before the system was handed over, covering default credential changes, network isolation, and access control verification.

Working on a government road infrastructure site also meant operating under strict safety procedures throughout; outdoor mounting at height, cable routing across an active checkpoint, and coordination with site personnel were all part of the execution. The project was closed with complete technical documentation covering the network topology, device inventory, IP plan, and test results, maintained and submitted as part of the official service record.
